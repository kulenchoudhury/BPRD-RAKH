History & Heritage of Barpeta Road Rash Mandir

Discover the decades of divine legacy, master craftsmanship, and the cultural spirit of Barpeta Road Rash Mandir and Raas Mahotsav in Assam.

History of Barpeta Road & Athiyabari Heritage

About 90 years ago, present-day Barpeta Road was a small village known as Athiyabari, surrounded by forests and wildlife. Only a few people lived here, including Koch, Bodo, Nepali, Chao-tal, and Muslim communities. Gradually, Bengali and Assamese people also settled here in search of livelihood. In 1927, when the railway station was established, the name “Barpeta Road” was given to the station by Lohit Chandra Nayak, the then Government Lawyer of Barpeta. However, the locality continued to be known as Athiyabari for some time. Following the establishment of the railway station, several Marwari businessmen came and settled in the area. At that time, the weekly market was held at Chengulia, about 4.5 km west of the present market. In 1929, Dhaniram Talukdar, the then Chairman of the Barpeta Local Board, closed the Chengulia weekly market and established the market at its present location.

Historical Foundation • 1927–1929

Origin & Early Years of Barpeta Road Rash Mandir (1929)

The Barpeta Road Sarbojanin Rash Mandir is located in the heart of Barpeta Road and is dedicated to Lord Krishna. The temple was established in 1929, the same year that the market was shifted to its present location. It was built at the northwestern corner of the market and was initially constructed with thatched materials. With the establishment of the temple, the Athiyabari Barwari Rash Puja Committee was formed to organize the Rash Puja and Rash Mela. The committee served from 1929 to 1962. Its office bearers included President Ramlal Boksi (Station Master), Secretary and Treasurer Bhupen Bhattacharya, and members Vinod Bihari Raharai (Guagacha), Pokhanmal Agarwal, Jatindra Narayan Biswas, Mahesh Chandra Pathak (Mouzadar), Hargobind Das (Hut Mahaldar), Dr. Ashwini Majumder, and others. In 1963, the first committee was dissolved and a second executive committee was formed for a two-year term, with Bhupen Bhattacharya as President and Antim Singh of Bagurapara as Secretary. During this period, the name of the Athiyabari Barwari Rash Puja Committee was changed to the Barpeta Road Sarbojanin Rash Utsav Committee.

After the dissolution of the second executive committee, a third executive committee was formed with Mathura Mohan Hitoishi as President, Nilkant Mandal as Vice-President, Dayamay Mandal as Secretary, and Jitendra Bhattacharya as Treasurer. During the earlier Rash festivals, the celebrations mainly included Kabigan, Krishna Leela, local Yatra performances, tea stalls, and a few shops selling goods. During this period, seven pairs of Radha-Krishna idols were installed inside the temple. Cultural programmes such as Bardhulia Yatra of Kamrup, Ojapali, Naam from Barpeta, Nataraj Opera of Pathsala, and Yatra performances from Hajo, Maruwa, Mugkuchi, and Kolkata were also organized. The Rash Mela featured puppet shows, magic exhibitions, zoos, motorcycle races inside the pond, local shops, and stalls selling various goods from outside Assam. In 1966–67, during Mathura Mohan Hitoishi’s tenure, a new concrete temple building was constructed, replacing the old structure. The temple was beautifully illuminated with electric lights during the Rash festival.

Rash Mahotsav Festival, Cultural Growth & Legacy

After the death of Mathura Mohan Hitoishi, a new executive committee was formed in 1990, with Anant Deuri as President and Madan Talukdar as Secretary. The period from 1991 to 1996 is widely regarded as an important chapter in the development of the Barpeta Road Rash Festival and Rash Mandir. During this period, several leaders served the committee, including Khargeshwar Nath, Hemen Das, Karnadhar Oja, Chanmohan Das, Phulen Das, and Anilananda Pathak. Their leadership and dedication contributed significantly to the development of the festival and the temple. A magnificent concrete building was constructed in front of the temple, followed by the construction of a dining hall and office facilities to meet the growing needs of the temple and festival.

However, during this period of development, tragedy struck on December 26, 1996, when an assailant entered the shop of Agarwal and Company on Station Road at around 7:15 p.m. and shot dead the owner. Following the incident, the district authorities did not permit the 69th Rash Festival to be held. Nevertheless, the tradition continued, and the 70th Rash Festival was celebrated in the city. In 1998, the festival management committee consisted of President Phulen Das, Secretary Sunanda Talukdar, Assistant Secretaries Kuljit Patwari and Moon Das, and Cultural and Souvenir Secretary Kulen Choudhury. This marked the beginning of a new chapter in the history of the Barpeta Road Sarbojanin Rash Festival. In 2003, the Platinum Jubilee of the Rash Festival was celebrated through the collective efforts and sacrifices of scholars, social workers, and various institutions. The celebration was successfully conducted under the leadership of a committee headed by social worker Binimay Mukharji as President and Bidyananda Choudhury as Secretary. A commemorative souvenir titled *Vrindavan* was also published on the occasion, preserving the history and heritage of this cherished festival.

DEVOTEE INQUIRIES

Frequently Asked Questions

Essential information about Barpeta Road Rash Mandir, Raas Mahotsav 2026, timings, and history.

What is Barpeta Road Sarbojanin Rash Mandir?

Barpeta Road Sarbojanin Rash Mandir is a historic Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Krishna, located in Main Bazar, Barpeta Road, Assam. It is the focal institution for the 98-year-old tradition of Sri Krishna's Raas Mahotsav in the region.

When was Barpeta Road Rash Mandir established?

The temple was established in 1929, the same year the weekly market was shifted from Chengulia to its present location. The initial thatched shrine was organized by the Athiyabari Barwari Rash Puja Committee, succeeded in 1966–67 by a permanent concrete temple building.

What is the Barpeta Road Rash Mahotsav?

Barpeta Road Rash Mahotsav is an annual 15-day celebration of Radha Krishna's divine Leela. It features life-sized clay idol exhibitions, daily Naam-Kirtan, Sandhya Aarti, cultural folk performances (such as Ojapali and Yatra), and community Mahaprasad distribution.

Where is Barpeta Road Rash Mandir located?

The Mandir is situated in Main Bazar, Barpeta Road, Barpeta District, Assam, PIN 781315. It is approximately 500 meters from the Barpeta Road Railway Station at geographic coordinates 26.5034° N, 90.9698° E.

What are the daily Darshan and Aarti timings?

Gates open at 05:30 AM with Mangala Aarti at 07:00 AM. The temple is closed for Madhyanha Bhog between 12:00 PM and 02:00 PM, re-opening at 02:10 PM. Sandhya Maha Aarti takes place daily at 07:00 PM.
